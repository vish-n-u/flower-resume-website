How Long Should a Resume Be in 2026?

One page or two? The best-known studies point in opposite directions. Here's what each one measured, and a simple way to decide for your own resume.

By the Flower Resume team · Published November 24, 2026 · 9 min read · 11 sources

"Keep it to one page" is probably the most repeated resume rule there is. It's also one of the most disputed. The two best-known pieces of data on resume length point in opposite directions, and both are worth understanding before you start deleting your best work or padding a thin one.

Quick answer

Your situationRecommended length
Student, new grad, or under ~3 years of experience1 page
3–10 years, one career track1 page if it fits naturally; 2 pages if every line is relevant
10+ years, management, or technical depth2 pages (rarely more)
U.S. federal jobs (USAJOBS)2 pages maximum, enforced since Sept 2025
UK CV2 sides of A4 is standard; 1 is fine for graduates
Academic CV / research rolesAs long as your publications and grants require

The case for two pages: what recruiters say they prefer

In late 2018, resume-writing service ResumeGo ran a hiring simulation with 482 recruiters, hiring managers, HR professionals and executives. Each screened resumes for a range of roles. Half the resumes were one page (350–500 words) and half were two pages (700–850 words), for candidates with similar qualifications.

Of the 7,712 resumes participants chose to move forward, 5,375 were two pages long, which ResumeGo reports as recruiters being 2.3 times as likely to prefer the longer version. The preference grew with seniority:

Job levelPreference for 2-page over 1-page
Entry level1.4×
Mid level2.6×
Senior / managerial2.9×

Participants also spent about 2 minutes 24 seconds on one-page resumes and 4 minutes 5 seconds on two-page ones. More text meant more reading time, not less patience.

The case for one page: what actually gets interviews

Jobscan looked at the question differently. Instead of asking recruiters what they prefer, it compared resume length against real interview rates from its own application data, and published the results in September 2026. Splitting resumes into four groups (quartiles) from shortest to longest:

  • Entry level: the shortest quartile had a 17.9% interview rate; the longest had 2.8%. That's roughly six times better for the shortest resumes.
  • Mid-career: the shortest quartile actually did worst (3.8%). The second quartile did best (9.8%), and rates fell again as resumes got longer.
  • Senior: the shortest quartile looked strong (15.6%), but Jobscan notes the sample was too small to rely on.

Why the two studies disagree

They're measuring different things. ResumeGo measured stated preference in a simulation, where people knew they were in a study and had time to read. Jobscan measured outcomes in real applications, where recruiters are busy and, as Ladders' eye-tracking found, the first pass lasts about 7 seconds. A two-page resume can look more impressive when you're reading carefully, yet do worse when it has to survive a fast skim.

There's also a confound in the real-world data. People with longer resumes may differ in other ways, so length alone may not cause the difference. And both studies come from companies that sell resume services. Neither is peer-reviewed.

Read together, though, the pattern is consistent. Early in your career, shorter wins. With real experience, a full and relevant second page helps. Too short is a problem too: mid-career resumes in Jobscan's shortest group did worst of all.

How to decide for your resume: the relevance test

Instead of aiming for a page count, test every line against the job you're applying for:

  1. Would the hiring manager for this job care? If not, cut it, however proud you are of it.
  2. Is it evidence, or just description? "Managed social media" can go. "Grew Instagram from 2k to 41k followers in 10 months" stays.
  3. Is it recent enough to matter? Roles from 10–15+ years ago can usually shrink to one line each, or move to a short "Earlier experience" section.

If everything passes and it runs onto a second page, that's a legitimate two-page resume. If the second page is mostly padding, you have a one-page resume with extra words.

What to cut first

  • An "Objective" statement. Replace it with a 2–3 line summary aimed at the role.
  • "References available upon request".
  • Generic soft skills ("team player", "hard-working") listed without evidence.
  • Duties that every person in that role would have done.
  • Bullets beyond the 3–5 strongest for older roles.
  • High-school details once you have a degree or a few years of work.
  • A full street address. City and country (or state) is enough.

What not to do

  • Don't shrink everything to fit. Body text below about 10 pt and very narrow margins make the 7-second skim harder. Cut words instead.
  • Don't pad to reach two pages. Even the CEO behind the pro-two-page study warns against it (see below).
  • Don't leave a half-empty second page. UK graduate site Targetjobs suggests recruiters may prefer a full second page to one that runs out halfway. Either tighten to one page or make the second page count.

Special cases

U.S. federal jobs: two pages, strictly

Federal resumes used to run to five pages or more. That changed under the Office of Personnel Management's Merit Hiring Plan. Since September 27, 2025, USAJOBS only accepts resumes of two pages or fewer. OPM's guidance applies the limit to competitive and excepted service announcements posted on USAJOBS or other platforms. A federal resume still has to show you meet the minimum qualifications, so the relevance test matters even more here.

UK and CV-based markets

In the UK, two sides of A4 is the standard expectation for most CVs. Recruitment firm Reed calls two pages the sweet spot for most professionals, and graduate careers site Targetjobs treats two pages as the maximum for a graduate CV, so one is fine if that's all you need. Academic CVs are the main exception and can be much longer.

Academic and research CVs

A full academic CV lists publications, grants, teaching and presentations, and has no fixed length. If you're applying for an industry role from academia, convert it to a 1–2 page resume focused on transferable results.
